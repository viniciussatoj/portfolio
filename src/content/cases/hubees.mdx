  I've worked inside design systems that were locked down too early and became too much of a constraint rather than a tool. Systems that are over-specified at launch don't survive contact with real product decisions.

<SectionTitle bar>Results_</SectionTitle>

Six months from kickoff to production launch, delivered in parallel with the rebrand campaign.

Beyond the 40% cut in design delivery time, the system changed how the team worked. Documented use cases made component decisions self-explanatory, and in many cases the alignment conversation stopped being necessary at all.

Finally, a happy surprise. With clear guidance on when and how to use the system, developers started prototyping improvements on their own, validating direction before pulling anyone else in. It only happened because the system was trustworthy enough to build on.
