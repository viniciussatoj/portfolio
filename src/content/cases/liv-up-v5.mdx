<SectionTitle bar>Problem_</SectionTitle>

Liv Up built its reputation on curated, healthy frozen meals. During COVID we added traditional groceries, and the catalog quadrupled. The new items were well reviewed. They just weren't being bought enough.

Customers who bought a wider variety stayed longer, so low variety was two problems at once: missing revenue and an early churn signal.

<SectionTitle bar>Discovery_</SectionTitle>

Every week the squad met for Coffee with Numbers, a review of the KPIs and of whether any recent delivery had moved the numbers. I started the same way here: with the PM and our data analyst, I traced the path from opening the app to a completed order, before and after groceries launched.

New customers were buying groceries without trouble but older customers were not.

<SectionTitle bar>Design_</SectionTitle>

Five decisions took us from a failed first version to the list that shipped. Each one answers a problem the previous one left open.

<Pair aN="1st iteration" aHeading="Start with a simple favorites feature" bN="2nd iteration" bHeading="Put recommendations inline next to related items">
<Fragment slot="a-text">

The first version was a single Favorites list, kept deliberately small to test adoption before committing to a costlier feature.

It revealed two problems. A favorite list incentivized users to not look anywhere else or users didn’t have the drive to build their favorite list.

</Fragment>

Better, but how could we suggest produce items for someone who never bought any before?

<Story n="3rd iteration" heading="Name it the way customers do and give them a head start">

In the interviews, nobody said “favorites”. They said “my shopping list”.

The action stays a heart, but the section moves the mindset from “things I like” to “things I'm going to buy”. It opens seeded with items from past orders, so nobody starts from a blank screen.

<Fragment slot="media">
<ScreenStage images={[{ src: listEmpty, alt: "Shopping list empty state, seeded with items from past orders" }]} />

</Story>

<Story n="4th iteration" heading="Keep the rest of the catalog one tap away" flip>

The shopping list solved a user need but it risked being too efficient, leading the user to ignore new products.

Recommendations from purchase history also can't suggest what someone has never bought, and nobody was buying cheese before groceries existed.

Four shortcuts above the list open paths into the wider catalog without forcing anyone to explore.

<SectionTitle bar>What I would Improve_</SectionTitle>

I’m proud of how we made users’ lives easier while reaching Liv Up’s business goal, but in retrospect I see shopping lists fully made by Liv Up as a natural evolution of this feature.

Users who like to browse are already browsing. If we recommended shopping lists with roughly the same size and cost the user is used to purchasing, sliding a few new recommendations shouldn’t be too radical. It may ease them into trying something new just by inertia of not removing items from a shopping list.
